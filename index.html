Frequently Asked Questions

Parent questions we hear every week

What are the signs of child sleep apnea?
Loud, frequent snoring, pauses in breathing during sleep, restless tossing and turning, mouth breathing, night sweats, and unusually tired or irritable behavior during the day are all common signs of child sleep apnea. If your child shows several of these, an ENT evaluation can determine what's causing it.
How is pediatric sleep apnea diagnosed and treated?
We start with a full airway exam and a review of your child's sleep symptoms, and may recommend a sleep study to confirm the diagnosis. Treatment depends on the cause — often enlarged tonsils and adenoids — and can range from monitoring and allergy management to a tonsillectomy or adenoidectomy in more significant cases.
When should my child see an ENT for snoring?
Occasional light snoring during a cold is usually nothing to worry about. But snoring that happens most nights, is loud, or comes with pauses in breathing is worth having evaluated — it's one of the clearest early indicators of pediatric sleep apnea.
Do enlarged tonsils and adenoids cause sleep apnea in kids?
Yes — enlarged tonsils and adenoids are the most common cause of obstructive sleep apnea in children, since they can physically narrow the airway during sleep. We evaluate their size and impact on breathing as part of every sleep apnea workup.
